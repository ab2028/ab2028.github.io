Avis et Carmina
Latin prose composition · 2026

Latin

Omnes aliae aves unam avem propter pennas pallidas deridebant, dicentes eam indignam amicitia inter eas. Avis sola quaerebat cur tam acerbe iudicaretur et quid facere posset ut amicitiam tandem mereret. Putavit, si verbis pulchris uteretur, vitia sua obtegere posset. Itaque unam ex suis pennis evulsit, in atramento tinxit, atque pro calamo usus est. Hac penna carmen scripsit.

English. All the other birds were mocking one bird on account of his dull feathers, saying that he was unworthy of friendship among them. Alone, the bird asked why he was judged so harshly and what he was able to do to finally get friendship. He thought that if he used beautiful words, he could hide his ugly flaws. And so, he plucked out one of his own feathers, dipped it in ink, and used it as a pen. With this feather he wrote a poem.

Cum omnes aves carmen laudarent, tamen non quaererent quis id scripisset. Ita avis aliud scripsit, atque aliud. Quotiens scribebat, unam pennam evellebat, donec corpus eius nudum esset.

English. Although all the birds praised the poem, still they did not ask who had written it. So the bird wrote another, and another. Each time he plucked a feather to write, until his body was bare.

Cum tandem corpus eius deplumis ante eas staret eumque poetam esse ostenderet, aves credere noluerunt. Dicebant poetam tam laudatum talem speciem habere non posse, atque verba a deis orta esse. Una ex eis clamavit, “Saltem antea pennas habebas; nunc ne volare quidem potes.” Carmina tenuerunt atque avem expulerunt.

English. When at last his featherless body stood before them, and revealed him to be the poet, the birds refused to believe him. They said that a poet so admired could not have such an appearance, and that the words had come from the gods. One of them cried, “At least before, you had feathers; now you cannot even fly.” They kept the poems and drove the bird away.
